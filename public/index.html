King’s Guard Artillery 70347
Assemble, collapse, look closer

        
The 77 vehicle elements of the King's Guard Artillery, placed step by step from the official instructions and drawn from real part geometry. Then a wave function collapse gets the same bag and builds other vehicles. Every joint it makes has to be one the booklet makes, and every build must pass a check for overlaps, forced studs and parts that couldn't actually be put in. Minifigures, the hammer, the spear and the six spare shots are left out.

        
The original is built in the booklet's order, one step at a time; each part slides in along the one line that is free when it arrives. The wave function works on a grid of studs and plates and may only stack pairs the booklet stacks. A second pass follows the booklet's step order and docks the three sub-assemblies (front axle with its cambered wheels, rear wheel with the lances, six-shooter) through the same joints the instructions use. Endless keeps doing that, one vehicle after another.

Sheet B-02 · The wave, layer by layer
Coloured cell: a brick is decided. Red wash: still undecided, darker is more options. Outline only: outside the envelope.

        

          

          

            
Every cell of a stud-by-plate grid may hold nothing or one fragment of a studded part. A fragment says which part, which turn, and which cell of that part it is. Two rules do all the work.

            
same part   fragments must touch their own kind:
            a 1×4 slope's cell 2 sits between cells 1 and 3
stud clutch a cell above a different part is legal only
            if the cell below has an open stud, this cell
            is the underside of its part, and the booklet
            joins those two kinds of element by a stud
            somewhere; a slope top forces air above it

            
Cells pick the fragment with the least entropy, weighted by the parts left in the box and by which parts touched in the original. Contradictions backtrack. Stage 2 walks the booklet's step order: loose Technic parts go wherever the rulebook allows, and the three sub-assemblies are turned any of 24 ways and docked through the joint the booklet gives them. Anything that fails the build check is taken out again and tried elsewhere.

Sheet B-03 · Build check
Runs on whatever is on the stage.

        

          

          

            
A build passes when all of these hold. Overlap: no two solid bodies share space (hollow-aware boxes, axles and pins included). Stud jam: a stud only ever enters a matching socket, never a wall, a beam or another stud. Weak grip: nothing three studs long hangs off a single stud. Shaft depth: an axle or pin reaches at least halfway into its hole. Locked in: the build can be taken apart one element at a time along straight lines, so it can also be put together. Rulebook: every joint is one the instructions make — exactly (creativity low) or the same kind of joint (creativity high). One piece: everything connects. Remixes are also turned to rest on their wheels where they can; the check says whether they do. Overlaps are measured on solid voxels of the real meshes, 0.8 mm across, studs included.

            
            

          

        

      


      

        
Sheet B-04 · Notes and sources

        

          
What this build is. The original on the stage follows the official building instructions of set 70347 (booklet 6187669, steps 1–37): the grey 2×4 Technic base, the 1×6 Technic brick across its back, the long pin and 3L axle with the two #3 angle connectors in front, the rugged rear wheel with its two lance axles, the two blue top sections built off the model, the wings, the T-beam and the six-shooter on the red axle, and the two cambered wheels in the connectors' angled holes. Positions come from reading the booklet's drawings, so where a drawing is ambiguous (the trim on top, which way a clip faces) the build takes the reading that passes every rule. The booklet was the only source; nothing was measured from a physical set.

          
Parts. The page 38 parts list of the booklet, less the minifigure parts, the stone monster's hammer (1×1 brick with two studs, 1×1 slope, pin with bar), the spear and six of the fourteen trans-orange shots, which lie loose next to the vehicle in step 37. The parts list shows five black 1×1 round plates with open stud, but the steps only use four; the fifth is left out.

          
Geometry. Every shape is a mesh converted from the LDraw part library, hidden faces removed, stored as 16-bit integers in this file. Where LDraw has a close relative rather than the exact element, the table says substitute next to the file name. Connection points (studs, sockets, pin, axle, bar and clip holes) are read from the same files. The six-shooter's barrels are modelled as six sockets for the shots.

          
Rules. LEGO keeps an internal list of building techniques its designers may not use, mostly because they stress or deform elements. The build check encodes the ones that apply to this bag: no forced or jammed studs, no parts wedged into cavities, no long parts hanging from a single stud, no fingertip-deep axles, and no element that could only be put in by passing through another. On top of that the remixer is held to the booklet as a rulebook: 52 distinct joints, Technic joints exactly as drawn, stud joints generalised to element families.

          
Colours. From set uses the set's colours. Random repaints each element type in a colour from a list of real, current LEGO colours. Black & white is the plain ink drawing.
